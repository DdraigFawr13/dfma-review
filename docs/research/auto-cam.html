Auto-CAM for DFM: what Xometry does and what we can use

Research for D-083 and D-084, 2026-11-11. Five web surveys plus checks of the cloned code in this session. Morgan made its proposals the plan on 2026-11-11 (D-084).

Answer. No open-source program goes from a solid model to setups, operations and toolpaths without a person picking the operations. We don't need one. You asked for CAM only as a DFM aid: find all the material to cut and check that the tools can reach it. Two methods do that without recognising any feature, and both run in the browser:

  1. Removal volume: stock minus part, with the OpenCascade boolean we already ship. Every bit of material to cut shows up, named or not.
  2. Tool reach: one height map per setup direction, offset by each tool's shape and holder ("inverse offset", Inui 2003). Where the lowest point a tool can reach sits above the part, material is left behind. Protolabs shows this as its "as-milled" view.

The best open code to help is OpenCAMLib, which already builds to WASM; we would use it to check our height maps. Manifold (Apache-2.0, WASM) can measure the volume left over. FreeCAD's adaptive clearing is worth porting only if we later want time from real toolpaths.

Xometry does not use auto-CAM. Its patent prices with machine-learning models trained on "recorded manufacture times and CNC operations of known physical objects". Its DFM thresholds are published in its design guides. We match 7 of its checks and lack 17.

What the app does today (read in this repo)

  • Stock is the part's own bounding box, with no allowance (web/src/engine/analyze.ts:160-163, Morgan 2026-11-08). Nothing computes the removal volume (grep of web/src/engine for stock, removal and BRepAlgoAPI_Cut: the cut appears only in port tests).
  • Features come from face patterns: holes, pockets, walls, turning (web/src/engine/features/). A shape outside that list is never seen, which matches the engineer's point that features are missed.
  • Setups are the fewest of the six axis directions (±X ±Y ±Z) that reach every recognised hole and pocket (web/src/engine/plan.ts:1-4). Tools are picked per feature. There is no cycle time yet.
  • 15 DFM rules (web/src/engine/rules/).

1. How instant quoters work

  • Xometry: machine learning on past jobs. Patent US10281902B2 predicts "set-up time, cycle time, number of Computer Numerical Control (CNC) operations" from geometric descriptors ("a number of manufacture tool directions", "non-planar surface area", "a number of holes") and point-cloud shape histograms. The models are random forests, extra-trees and logistic regression. No toolpaths appear in the text read.
  • Proto Labs: real toolpaths. Patent US8239284B2: "tool selection and tool path computation". The quote may rest on "a total of estimated durations of material removal". The user sees an "as-milled" view with the material left behind highlighted in blue.
  • Toolpath.com: real machining strategies; "the fewest number of setups", workholding "access and collision aware".
  • Paperless Parts: geometry, not toolpaths: "setup count", "setups, holes, pockets, and 3D features", "volume removal that drive costs"; "52 different manufacturability warnings".
  • aPriori: "volumetric material removal features", which drive roughing tool size and cycle time.
  • Hubs: machine learning against "millions of previously manufactured parts". CloudNC CAM Assist is a CAM add-in for Fusion, NX and SolidCAM, not a quoter.
  • What needs past-job data (inference): market price, margin, lead time and a shop's real cycle times. Setups, reach, removal volume and DFM warnings need only geometry.

2. Xometry's DFM checks against ours

Xometry doesn't publish what its upload check runs; its site says only that DFM results "will be displayed here". The thresholds below are from its design guides: the 2023 Europe guide (G23) and a 2018 US copy (G18). I re-read four of them in the extracted text: 0.794 mm, "six times", 75%, and "4 times for pockets".

Covered by our rules (we still compare the numbers): inside corner radius (theirs: 1.3 × the tool radius, depth ÷ radius ≤ 4; corner_radius) · cavity depth ("greater than six times deep than they are wide are considered too deep", ideal under 4 × width; pocket_depth) · hole depth (1:4 ideal, 1:10 limit; hole_depth) · drill sizes (0.1 mm steps to 13 mm, then 0.5 mm; hole_size) · wall thickness (0.794 mm metal, 1.5 mm plastic; thin_wall) · undercuts (undercut) · inaccessible areas (snippet only; access).

Xometry checks we lack (17):

  • Thread length over 2 × diameter.
  • Blind tapped hole without an unthreaded length of 0.5 × diameter at the bottom.
  • Thread smaller than M2.
  • Custom thread, which needs "a manual quote review".
  • Blind hole without a 25% depth allowance.
  • Edge hole with less than 75% inside the part.
  • Hole or pocket closer than 0.8 mm to a wall (double in plastic).
  • Hole entering a sloped or curved face.
  • Hole crossing a cavity.
  • Curved hole.
  • Floor fillet not smaller than the wall fillet.
  • Corner radius equal to a standard tool radius.
  • Feature under 0.5 mm, or needing a tool under 2.5 mm.
  • Part bigger than the machine: mill 80 × 48 × 24 in; turn 62 in long, 32 in diameter; height over 24 in needs manual review.
  • Text smaller than 20 pt, or raised rather than cut in.
  • Tolerance tier: auto-quoted to ±0.001 in in metal, tighter needs review.
  • Many different radii and thread sizes, which mean more tool changes (advice, no number).

Ours with no Xometry counterpart: blend, hole and thread mismatch, bore depth, and the turning rules. Severities are not published; uploading a test part to Xometry would show them. That is publishing a file to an outside service, so it needs Morgan's OK (SAFE-01).

3. Open-source CAM engines

Ranked for our case: a browser app, a DFM aid, no G-code. Licences are from the licence files in clones made this session.

  1. OpenCAMLib (aewallin/opencamlib): C++, with an emscripten build (src/emscriptenlib/emscriptenlib.cpp:165, BatchDropCutter) and npm @opencamlib/opencamlib (last published 2023). Drop-cutter and waterline for cylinder, ball, bull and cone tools. Licence file LGPL-2.1, but the npm package.json:30 says LGPL-3.0; the two need settling. It works on meshes and has no holder model. Use: check our height maps against it.
  2. FreeCAD CAM, Adaptive clearing (FreeCAD): libarea/Adaptive.cpp is 4,360 lines and includes only Clipper2 and the C++ standard library (checked). LGPL-2.1-or-later. It would compile to WASM; the step-down logic around it is about 2,000 lines of Python to port. A person picks every operation. Time comes from path length against feed. Use: later, if we want toolpath-based time.
  3. Manifold (elalish/manifold): Apache-2.0, WASM on npm (manifold-3d). Fast mesh booleans with MinkowskiSum and MinkowskiDifference (manifold.h:244-245, checked). Use: subtract the space a tool can sweep from the removal volume to measure what is left. Kiri:Moto uses it for stock simulation.

Ruled out or reference only:

  • Kiri:Moto: a browser CAM. Its licence file says MIT, but 256 source files say "All Rights Reserved" (counted); don't take its code without the author's written word. It reads meshes, and a person picks the operations.
  • Clipper2 (BSL-1.0, permissive): 2D offsets. A dependency, not a CAM.
  • GPL, so not for our bundle: CAMotics (no release since 2019), PyCAM (stale since 2021), BlenderCAM/Fabex (runs inside Blender), jscut (2D only).
  • HeeksCNC/libarea: stale; FreeCAD carries its successor.
  • ocp-freecad-cam: a Python wrapper, "fairly experimental".
  • No academic auto-CAM with released code was found.

Licences (inference, not legal advice): LGPL obligations start on distribution; use inside one company is generally not distribution (FSF FAQ). We already ship OCCT under LGPL-2.1, so an LGPL module kept as its own replaceable .wasm follows the same pattern. Any new runtime dependency needs Morgan's OK (SAFE-01).

4. Finding all the material to cut

  • Stock minus part (BRepAlgoAPI_Cut, in opencascade.js already). Split the result into separate solids and take each one's volume (BRepGProp). This finds every region to cut. It doesn't say which tool or direction cuts it.
  • Splitting the removal volume into features (Woo's ASV 1982; Kim and Wilde's ASVP 1992; Sakurai and Dave 1995–96; Woo and Sakurai 2002): no public code found. Most of it is for flat-faced parts, and on filleted parts the cell count and boolean failures grow (inference). Use it only to group what is left over, not to decide reach.
  • Our feature recognition stays, to name regions and pick tools. Any removal volume no feature explains becomes a finding of its own: "unrecognised material, X in³, here".

5. Checking tool reach

  • Inverse offset on a height map: Inui, "Fast inverse offset computation using polygon rendering hardware", CAD 2003. Render the part from a setup direction, then offset by the tool's shape. The result is the lowest height the tool tip can reach at each point. It works for any feature shape.
  • Holder and reach: Kaneko, Yamauchi and Horio, IJAT 8(3) 2014. Inverse offset plus shadow mapping shows the unreachable area and the shortest shank needed, in "a few dozen ms" on a GPU.
  • Other methods, no code found: GPU ray tracing (Inui 2021) needs ray-tracing cores, which browsers can't use. Voxel collision checks (Chen et al.; NSF). Behandish et al. (arXiv 1805.07035), which includes the holder.
  • Setup directions beyond ±X ±Y ±Z: visibility maps on the Gauss sphere (Gan, Woo and Tang 1994; Chen, Chou and Woo 1993). These pick 3+2 orientations from face normals. No open code found.
  • Grid size (computed here): a 12 in part at 0.005 in spacing is 2,400 × 2,400 = 5.8 million points, 22 MiB as float32. 0.0025 in spacing is 23 million points, 88 MiB. That is enough to see a 0.01 in feature at 2–4 points across (inference). GPU speed in the browser is not measured.
  • Limits: a height map can't see undercuts or walls seen edge-on, so steep walls need the side directions (or tri-dexels later). Features under 2 points wide are lost. Headless Chromium in CI may lack WebGL (inference, to test).
  • Reporting (D-001): per leftover region, give volume, footprint, deepest gap, faces touched, the tool and direction tried, and why: tool too wide, flute too short, or holder hits. Also give "X % of the removal volume is reachable with this tool set". Mark faces with a hatch as well as a colour (APP-02).

6. Time, feeds and data

  • Time without toolpaths: Boothroyd and Dewhurst, "Early cost estimating in product design" (J. Manuf. Systems 1988), uses removal volume and shop rate. Shehab and Abdalla (RCIM 2001) is a feature-level cross-check. Both would need the removal volume from section 4.
  • No public answer key of parts with real cycle times or quotes was found. Nearest: Zenodo 17035762 (CC-BY-4.0): 10 STEP parts with G-code for single-setup 3-axis milling, no times. Timing its G-code with gcodestat (Unlicense, acceleration-aware) would give our own key. That key is ours, so we can fail it (REP-05).
  • Feeds and speeds: no openly licensed cutting-data table found. CUTDATA is paid. The FreeCAD FeedsAndSpeeds addon (LGPL-2.1) doesn't state its data source.
  • Tool library formats: FreeCAD toolbit JSON (documented) and LinuxCNC tool.tbl are open. Fusion's format "is not published and could change".
  • DFM rule sources: the Hubs guide (cavity ≤ 4 × width, internal radius ≥ ⅓ depth) and Protolabs' guide. CADEX MTK's published check list (commercial) is a useful coverage checklist.
  • Datasets: AAGNet/MFInstSeg has over 60,000 STEP files with machining-feature labels; code MIT, data licence not stated. Fusion 360 Gallery is "only for non-commercial research purposes".

7. Inventor and Vault

  • Vault Data API (REST), since Vault 2025.2 (APS blog). It works "via a Vault server address or a Vault Gateway address"; the focus is on reading, and writing is limited (snippet). A browser app calling it needs CORS set up on the Vault server's IIS.
  • Vault Gateway requires "A subscription to Vault Professional" (Autodesk help).
  • Vault Data Standard is free and PowerShell-based. It runs inside the Vault desktop client (Workgroup or Professional): a desktop route, not a web one.
  • Inventor CAM and Fusion CAM APIs can generate toolpaths. Both need a paid desktop seat, so they can't run in our server-free app and would lock us to one vendor (D-001; inference).
  • What Vault edition and version the company runs decides the route. That needs IT's answer.

The plan (Morgan, 2026-11-11: "make this the plan for next steps"; D-084)

  1. P1, removal volume first: stock (bounding box plus a set allowance, or the user's stock) minus part. Show the volume of each region, and flag any region no feature explains. This fixes "doesn't find all features" without new recognisers.
  2. P2, a reach check: GPU height maps per setup direction with each tool's diameter, corner radius, flute length and a simple holder. Report material left behind per tool and direction, with its size. Check it against OpenCAMLib on the NIST parts.
  3. P3, the missing Xometry checks, as new rules with test parts. Thread checks and wall distance first, since they need only features we already find.
  4. P4, later: time from removal volume (Boothroyd–Dewhurst), checked against the Zenodo G-code times. FreeCAD Adaptive in WASM only if that falls short.
  5. Not proposed: Kiri:Moto code (licence conflict), GPL engines, Inventor or Fusion CAM (paid seat, lock-in), machine learning (D-001).

New dependencies (OpenCAMLib, Manifold) need your OK first (SAFE-01). The next step after your answers is a plan with planning-interview (D-083).
