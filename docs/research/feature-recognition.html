Steps, slots and open pockets

2026-11-10 · for Morgan · how CAD/CAM and DFM tools find, name and machine these features, and how the app could add them

The answer: the tools agree on what the features are. A step is a floor with one wall (a shoulder): open on two sides. A slot is a channel: two walls that face each other, a floor, and ends that are open, flat or round. A pocket has walls all round (closed) or is missing some walls (open). The STEP machining-feature standard, Cimatron, CAD Exchanger and Fusion use these same words.

What separates them is countable on our model: how many of the floor's edges have a wall, and whether the open edges lead out of the part. Our pocket finder already does the hard part (it finds floors, walls and the open-to-outside test). Today it throws away every floor that has an open side. The proposals keep those floors and name them step, slot or open pocket.

The shop effect is real: an open side lets the cutter enter from outside (no ramp or pre-drilled hole), a narrow slot means full-width slotting (lower depth per pass, chip packing), and T-slots, keyseats and dovetails need special cutters. 5 proposals at the bottom. None is decided.

What the app does today (read in this repo)

  • A pocket needs a wall on every outer edge of its floor, or an edge "cut by another feature inside the part"; otherwise the floor is dropped (service/dfma/features.py:895-929, _pocket_walls: else: return None).
  • The "break" probe (features.py:813-839, _Enclosure.inside) casts 5 rays in the floor's plane from just past a wall-less edge. All must hit the part again. Its docstring: "A slot or step that opens to the outside, or a through slot that runs out of the part, lets a ray escape." So the app already detects the open side; it uses it only to reject.
  • A found pocket gets one open direction, its floor normal (features.py:1319-1321); the plan groups jobs by open_dirs (service/dfma/plan.py:108).
  • The end mill is the largest that fits the corner radius and the narrowest width (service/dfma/tools.py:58-63, pick_end_mill). For a slot, that is a cutter as wide as the slot: full slotting.
  • Scoring: MFCAD++ slots (classes 5-7 through, 17-19 blind) and steps (8-10 through, 20-22 blind) are not scored; any call on them counts against "0 wrong" (tests/grading/grade.py:35-45, D-018). D-019 lets slot faces shaped like a pocket beside a step count as pockets (20 of 40 at the time). LESSONS "recognition" ×1: a slot rule fitted MFCAD++ labels and removed 5 real pockets on EDMR parts.
  • D-021 already finds T-slot-like undercuts in pockets (rule undercut); set 2 has T-slots and dovetails, labelled 6, 17, 18 (D-018).

2. How tools find them (methods that suit deterministic code)

  • Attributed adjacency graph (AAG): faces are nodes, shared edges are arcs marked convex or concave; a feature is a matching subgraph. Joshi and Chang (1988) list pockets, slots, steps and blind steps; their version handled plane faces only. Weakness: "The intersection of features causes an explosion in the number of possible feature patterns." Joshi & Chang, CAD 20(2); Wikipedia, Feature recognition.
  • Convex-only pruning: a face whose edges are all convex is not part of a pocket or slot; Analysis Situs removes those first, and its author calls the AAG "a supplementary data structure". Quaoar blog (search snippet).
  • Hints: look for the trace a feature leaves even after another cuts it. "Presence of two opposing planar faces is a hint for potential existence of a slot feature." Better with interacting features; weakness: "a huge number of traces that won't lead to valid features." Wikipedia (Vandenbrande and Requicha; Han and Requicha).
  • Volume decomposition: take stock minus part (the delta volume), split it into cells by extending faces, and join cells into "maximal volumes" with no concave edge. Weaknesses: face extension has global effects and joining cells is heavy. Woo 2002 (DOI) (search summary).
  • Open side, by tools: Cimatron calls a pocket open when "some walls may be absent", and a through pocket open only "if a pocket with an area is created when connecting its open ends". Fusion needs the open edge declared, and chains of an open pocket with an island "extend to the stock boundary". Cimatron; Autodesk forum (search snippet); Fusion 2D Pocket.
  • Learning methods (context only; runtime AI is excluded by D-001): UV-Net, Hierarchical CADNet (made MFCAD++), AAGNet (made MFInstSeg, 62,495 parts, 24 classes), BRepFormer (99.62% on MFInstSeg, its own table). BRepFormer. They need labelled training data and give no measured evidence per call, which D-001 asks for. inference

  • MFCAD++: 59,655 parts, 24 classes, 3-10 features each, made by a generator in 5 groups (steps, slots, through, non-through, transition). CAD deep-learning survey. Labels are per face; we already read them (grade.py). Steps 8-10 and 20-22, slots 5-7 and 17-19. No open pocket. Labels come from the generator, not the shape (LESSONS "recognition").
  • MFInstSeg (AAGNet): over 60,000 STEP files with instance labels, 24 classes. Class list not confirmed on an opened page. BRepFormer.
  • Fusion 360 Gallery segmentation: 35,858 bodies labelled by the modelling operation (extrude, cut, fillet, chamfer, …), not by machining feature. Not useful here. GitHub (search snippet).
  • UMD parts already in the repo (tests/edmr/models/UMD, real labels from .fts files, counted in base files only): 35 steps in 10 parts, 27 slots in 12, 15 opened pockets in 6, 9 pockets in 3. Caution: bracket1-4, castle and ex1 crash OpenCascade's reader (docs/nist/CATALOG.md:40), so the usable open-pocket parts are only AES94 (count by part not checked).
  • NIST PMI parts: not checked feature by feature this session. The milled CTC/FTC parts likely hold steps and open slots inference, unchecked; confirm by running a finder and looking, not from memory.

5. Proposals (not decided; for Morgan)

  1. Keep open floors instead of dropping them. Where _pocket_walls returns None because an edge has no wall and the break probe's rays escape, record the floor with its walls and its open edges. Name it by the table above: step, through slot, blind slot, open pocket. Gives each a depth, width and open directions (the floor normal plus each open side's outward direction). Risk: new calls on MFCAD++ faces now scored as "wrong" (D-018 target of 0); it needs D-018 amended so step and slot calls are scored against classes 5-10 and 17-22, with their own target.
  2. Plan effect: side entry. An open pocket or step adds the open sides to open_dirs, so the plan can reach it from the side or the top, and a closed pocket gets a note that it needs a ramp, helix or pilot hole (JLC, CIMCO). Gives fewer forced setups. Risk: a tool that enters from the side still needs reach to the far wall.
  3. Slot cutter and passes. For a slot, choose an end mill smaller than the slot (Sandvik: undersized, rough then finish; trochoidal ≤ 70% of width) instead of pick_end_mill's full width, or keep full width and count passes at D-052's per-pass depth. New finding: a slot deeper than 6 × its width (Protolabs) or with a tool L:D over 3 (DFMXpress). Risk: two sources, two limits; pick one with Morgan.
  4. Special cutters. Reuse D-021's undercut to name T-slot (neck under a head) and dovetail (walls leaning in) and add a tool load (tslot-<w>, dovetail-<deg>, as D-022 did for countersinks). Findings: a non-standard dovetail angle (not 45° or 60°, Hubs); undercut clearance under 4 × depth (Hubs). A keyway on a turned part: end mill for a closed end, keyseat cutter for an open one. Risk: keyways sit with S6 turning work, which is paused.
  5. Test set and scoring first. Before any rule: add tests/grading/sets/steps_slots (through and blind steps, open and closed slots with flat and round ends, open pockets on 1, 2 and 3 sides, a slot beside a step as D-019's guard), score all three tools through grade.py, and run NIST and EDMR first (LESSONS "recognition"). Use the UMD .fts labels on AES94 and the parts that read. Risk: MFCAD++ has no open-pocket label, so the UMD parts and our set are the only answer key for that call.
