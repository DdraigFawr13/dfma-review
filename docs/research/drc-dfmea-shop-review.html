DRC, DFMEA and shop review: what helps DFMA Review

Research for Morgan, 2026-11-06. These are proposals, not decisions. Nothing here changes S3 to S7.

Answer: four ideas are worth keeping. All of them fit later phases we already plan, and none needs AI:

  • waivers on a finding
  • shop rule profiles
  • a change review that re-measures the part
  • comments and sign-off per finding

Two ideas look attractive but break our rules: scoring FMEA risk automatically, and AI DFM.

My recommendation: build none of it now. Write the four into the later phases they belong to, so each phase's planning starts from them.

Keep: ideas that fit

History phaseWaive a finding, with who, when and why

Altium records a waiver on a design-rule violation with its date, author and reason. Every run still finds the violation and re-applies the waiver. Waived items leave the error list but stay in a separate report section.

My take: this is the most useful idea for shop reviews ("we know, the fit needs it"). It needs saved history: a waiver lost on page reload is worse than none. It also needs a finding id that survives a re-run (rule plus feature position). Our ids F1, F2… renumber when the findings change.

Sources: Altium: waiving DRC violations · Altium: resolving violations

Shop lists phaseShop profiles: one settings file per shop or process

SolidWorks DFMXpress groups its rules by process (mill/drill, turn, sheet metal, moulding). Its standard hole list has a tolerance and an "Enabled" box per size. Altium uses the most specific matching rule first and a catch-all at the bottom, and exports rule sets as files. aPriori keeps each machine's tooling and rules in an editable "digital factory".

My take: this is the shape of the planned "Morgan's own machine and tool lists" phase. Our thresholds.toml and tools.toml already hold limits per material, and every finding names its source. A shop profile would turn those two files into one exportable file per shop. A per-size "enabled" flag on drills answers "we don't stock that drill".

Sources: DFMXpress rules · DFMXpress settings · Altium rule priority · Altium rule export · aPriori digital factories

Compare phaseChange review: did the fix make the part weaker?

DRBFM (design review based on failure mode) lists what changed and the concerns each change raises, in separate designer and review-team columns. Onshape, Creo and CATIA measure wall thickness without FEA, by rays through the part or the largest ball that fits inside.

My take: this answers DFMEA point 2 in your screenshot, within our rules. Run every rule on rev A and rev B, then show:

  • findings added, removed and changed
  • how the thinnest wall, the smallest corner and the volume changed

It can't say whether the part is still strong enough under load. That needs FEA or a hand calculation, and the tool must never claim it.

Sources: ReliaSoft DRBFM · Onshape thickness analysis · Creo+ 3D thickness

History phaseComments and sign-off per finding, in the shared report

DFMPro reports list each issue, its place on the model and reference dimensions, for design and manufacturing to share. Its templates can be changed. Paperless Parts shows estimators the features and DFM warnings on the model.

My take: this is the "supplier and shop floor review" in your screenshot. Each finding would get a comment, a reviewer, a date and a decision (change, waive, ask). The PDF would carry them too. It needs saved history and a way to know who is commenting, so it waits for those phases.

Sources: DFMPro on G2 · DFMPro report templates · Paperless Parts viewer

Low priorityExport findings as rows for an FMEA

FMEA tools such as Relyence import rows from a spreadsheet. A finding can be a row with the feature, rule, measured value and limit, and the cause filled in from the rule's text.

My take: it's cheap once the report exists (S7), but only if your company keeps DFMEAs. Severity, occurrence and detection stay blank for the team to fill in. I found no DFM tool that does this (searched "DFM findings link DFMEA PFMEA", "import DRC results into FMEA rows").

Source: Relyence FMEA import

Already thereEditable severity and limits

aPriori groups findings as critical, major and minor, and users can change both the severity and the limit. Protolabs splits "required change" from "advisory".

My take: we already have this. Limits are editable per material in thresholds.toml, and high and medium play the parts of required and advisory. A third label would say the same thing twice. Making severity editable per rule is a small add if a shop manager asks for it.

Sources: aPriori design guidance (from a search snippet; the page refused us) · Protolabs manufacturing analysis

Avoid

Breaks D-001Scoring FMEA risk automatically

In the AIAG-VDA method, occurrence and detection depend on the controls in place and on field data. Those are a team's judgement, not measurements on the model. A score we invented would be a number without evidence.

Source: Quality Digest on AIAG-VDA FMEA

Breaks D-001AI DFM or AI-written FMEAs

Protolabs ProDesk and Flux advertise these. You asked for no AI at runtime.

Sources: ProDesk · Flux FMEA

AvoidRules written as scripts, or a check inside the CAD program

NX and DFMPro let users write rules as code. That means running other people's code on the server, which is hard to test and a security risk (my inference). A CAD plug-in would tie us to one CAD vendor and break the portability rule. Keep rule editing to numbers in the settings files.

Sources: NX validation · DFMPro in-house rules
